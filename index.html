At the Table

Sacred Food

Every meal means something. Nothing is eaten just because you're hungry.

Gold Wafer Communion
Before Big Decisions

A tiny cracker covered in real gold leaf and black truffle. You eat it slowly, in silence, before any big business decision. It's a reminder that every choice has a price.

The Great Feast
Once a Year

A 12-course dinner with the rarest ingredients in the world — real Wagyu beef, caviar, truffles. No phones. No outside guests. Members only.

The Three-Day Fast
Dec 29 – 31

At the end of the year, you eat almost nothing for three days — just water and plain broth. A little hunger makes you sharper and more focused on what matters.

The Sacred Wine
All Ceremonies

Only wine that's at least 20 years old is allowed at ceremonies. Opening a bottle is a whole event — two senior members must witness. Cheap wine is basically an insult.

The Heir's Broth
Passing the Torch

When someone is named the next head of the family, they drink a bowl of broth made from cattle that lived on the family land for seven years. It's about taking in everything built before you.

The Calendar

Five Holy Days

Five days a year that every member takes seriously. No exceptions.

January 1st
The Reckoning

Not a party. You spend the whole day alone — going over how you did last year. What grew? What didn't? What was a waste of time? Quiet, serious, personal.

Spring — First Day
The Gilded Ascension

When people who've grown their wealth enough move up in rank. You get a new robe — black with a gold lining. Your old one gets burned. It's a big deal.

Last Saturday of September
The Covenant Feast

The big annual gathering. Held at a private estate. Everyone wears a mask. No phones. You eat together, sit in silence, and listen to the names of the top-ranked members read aloud.

Second Friday of November
Day of Giving Face

Every member makes a public charity donation — but it has to carry your name. It's about making sure your name is attached to something lasting.

December 28th
The Night of Ledgers

You burn a piece of paper for every relationship, habit, or project that wasted your time this year. You let it go. Only the useful stuff comes with you into the new year.

Once Per Generation

The Inheritance Vigil

No outsiders allowed. Ever.

When a top-ranked member decides they're getting close to the end, they pick the person who will take over — and the handoff begins. Not with papers. Not with lawyers. With time.

For the first full year, the chosen person follows the elder everywhere. Every meeting. Every dinner. Every ceremony. But they can't speak on the elder's behalf. They just watch.

In the second year, the elder stops giving orders. They only ask questions. Every week they meet by candlelight and the elder says just one word: "More," "Less," or "Correct."

At some point, the elder gives the heir one personal object. A watch. A pen. A rock from somewhere that mattered. This is called the Animus. You never sell it. You never explain it to anyone outside the covenant.

When the elder passes, the heir wears the Animus every day for 30 days. On day 31, they sit in the elder's seat at the annual feast. No introduction needed. Everyone already knows.

The point of the Vigil isn't just to hand over money.
It's to hand over wisdom.

You have to learn someone's silences before you're ready to carry their name.
